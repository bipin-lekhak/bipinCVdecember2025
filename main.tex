    \section{Summary}
    \begin{onecolentry}
        PhD candidate building \textbf{scientific machine learning models of dynamical systems}: latent
        neural-ODE models that combine structural assumptions with data to predict how a system will respond to
        control inputs and interventions. The models separate a system's intrinsic dynamics from its response to
        inputs, are identifiable, and adapt to each individual system from its own history through
        meta-learning. Validated on physical systems observed through video, mechanistic simulators and clinical
        time series. Before the PhD, four years as a machine learning engineer shipping computer-vision and
        forecasting systems.
    \end{onecolentry}

    \section{Research Experience}

    \begin{twocolentry}{
        \textit{Rochester, NY}

        \textit{Aug 2022 – Present}}
        \textbf{Graduate Research Assistant}

        \textit{Computational Biomedicine Laboratory, RIT} (PI: Prof. Linwei Wang)
    \end{twocolentry}

    \vspace{0.10 cm}
    \begin{onecolentry}
        \begin{highlights}
            \item \textbf{Interventional latent dynamics (iASIDE), project lead and first author.} Developed a latent
            neural-ODE framework that predicts a system's trajectory under future control inputs or treatments. It
            separates intrinsic dynamics from the response to inputs, adapts both to each system from its history
            through amortized (feed-forward) meta-learning, and establishes identifiability of the learned
            dynamics. Gains over baselines are largest at long horizons and across heterogeneous systems.
            \item \textbf{Physics-based simulation.} Wrote a simulator of damped, forced pendulums under stochastic
            inputs, rendered as image sequences, to test whether models recover physical parameters from video.
            Also evaluated on a pharmacokinetic--pharmacodynamic simulator of tumour growth under therapy and on
            MIMIC-III intensive-care records.
            \item \textbf{Reusable research software.} Built the project's experiment framework: 10+ baselines
            (Causal Transformer, CRN, RMSN, MSM, G-Net, SCIP, TE-CDE, TESAR-CDE, ACTIN) on three benchmarks, with
            Hydra configuration, PyTorch Lightning, MLflow tracking, SLURM sweeps across GPU clusters, and
            regression tests that verified a migration from PyTorch 1.13 to 2.x.
            \item \textbf{Real-world sensor data} (NIH R01NR018301, with the University of Rochester Medical
            Center). Analysed multi-sensor time series from devices deployed in heart-failure patients' homes for
            90 days. Characterised deployment failures and missing data, built data dashboards, and
            predicted 21-day hospitalisation from 7-day windows (AUC 0.74).
            \item \textbf{Treatment-effect estimation over time} (work in progress, with S.\ Poudel). Temporal X- and
            R-learners that build on the separable latent dynamics to identify causal effects.
        \end{highlights}
    \end{onecolentry}
